Profile

My research interests lie in the field of Compact Object Astrophysics where I study Neutron Stars and Hybrid Stars. I aim to use multi-messenger observations of Gravitational Waves and Electromagnetic Signals in order to constrain theoretical models of the Nuclear Equation of State.

I am interested in developing computational simulations to study astrophysical systems, with a particular focus on neutron-star merger remnants. I am exploring these systems through both dynamical time-evolution and the computation of quasi-static equilibrium sequences, with the aim of connecting macroscopic remnant properties to the underlying microphysics and, ultimately, to observable multi-messenger signals.

My broader interests include studying the evolution of binary star systems in the common-envelope phase. Such systems are thought to play an important channel in the formation of Binary Neutron Stars. I am also interested in accretion disk physics and protostellar evolution and hope to explore these areas further.

Projects

  • Aug 2026 – Present
    Nuclear Equation of State: RMF/QHD Implementation in Neutron Stars
    • Studying the theoretical foundations of Quantum Hadrodynamics and Relativistic Mean Field theory as a framework for describing dense nuclear matter beyond saturation density.
    • Implementing a beta-equilibrated nuclear matter EOS incorporating the baryon octet coupled to σ, ω, and ρ mesons, with leptons (electrons and muons) enforcing charge neutrality, working toward a self-consistent mean-field numerical solver.
    • Actively extending the framework toward hyperon-rich matter and hybrid EOS construction incorporating a hadron-quark phase transition, with focus on the observational consequences for maximum mass and compactness.
  • Jun 2026 – Jul 2026
    Rotational Energy Budget of Supramassive Neutron Star Merger Remnants
    Supervised by Dr. Ritam Mallick, IISER-B Summer Project
    • Studied the theory of rotating neutron stars in general relativity and the turning point theorem, which identifies the onset of secular instability along constant-baryonic-mass sequences as the criterion for spin-down collapse of supramassive configurations.
    • Used the RNS code to construct sequences of rotating stellar configurations for supramassive neutron stars, parameterised by baryonic mass, spanning from the mass-shedding limit down to the turning point of instability.
    • Computed the extractable rotational kinetic energy along these sequences — the energy released as a supramassive remnant spins down toward collapse — and investigated to what extent an associated kilonova transient could be “super-charged” by this energy.
    • Compared the computed energy budget against the observed kilonova energy of GW170817, finding that if the post-merger remnant was supramassive, it must have been close to the supramassive limit.
  • Jun 2025 – Jul 2025
    Exploring Neutron Stars in R2 Gravity
    Supervised by Mr. Asim Kumar Saha and Dr. Ritam Mallick, IISER-B Summer Project
    • Studied f(R) theories of gravity as extensions of General Relativity; derived the modified gravitational field equations for the Starobinsky R2 model from the action principle, both by hand and via a generalised Mathematica pipeline.
    • Extended the Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff stellar structure equations to f(R) gravity, deriving the modified pressure, mass, and scalaron evolution equations governing hydrostatic equilibrium in a curved-spacetime framework.
    • Numerically integrated the modified stellar structure equations for a polytropic equation of state using a shooting method, mapping out mass-radius configurations under R2 gravity.
    • Encountered and diagnosed the well-known scalaron fine-tuning problem: the exterior solution is exponentially sensitive to the central value of the Ricci scalar, requiring precise initial conditions to produce physically regular solutions — a fundamental numerical pathology of the f(R) stellar structure problem.
  • Aug 2025 – Nov 2025
    Numerical Relativity in 1 Dimension
    Supervised by Mr. Hrishikesh Borthakur and Dr. Ritam Mallick, IISER-B Semester Project
    • Worked through the opening four chapters of Baumgarte and Shapiro’s Numerical Relativity, covering the 3+1 decomposition of Einstein’s equations, conformal reformulations, and the initial value problem.
    • Supplemented reading with hands-on simulation: used the GR1D code to evolve a spherically symmetric polytropic star (K = 100, Γ = 2), verifying equilibrium stability and developing familiarity with a production-level GR hydrodynamics framework.
